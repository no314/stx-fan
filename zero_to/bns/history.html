A history of BNS

The Bitcoin Naming System is older than most of the tools that use it: it started as a Namecoin identity directory in 2014, moved to Bitcoin in 2015, and has lived on Stacks since the 2021 genesis block. Every dated claim below carries its sources (APA style, references at the bottom); where a fact is chain-verifiable, the chain is the final arbiter.

Timeline

2014
MarchOneName launches as an open-source registrar and public directory for names on Namecoin's /u namespace, built by Muneeb Ali and Ryan Shea; this naming layer is what later becomes BNS. (CoinDesk, 2014a; Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b)
NovemberAfter the summer 2014 Y Combinator batch, Onename announces a 1.5 million dollar seed round from Union Square Ventures, SV Angel, and angels including Naval Ravikant and Barry Silbert. (CoinDesk, 2014b; VentureBeat, 2014)
2015
SeptemberThe names migrate from Namecoin to Bitcoin itself, anchored through OP_RETURN; roughly 40 BTC are burned in the public commitment that opens the .id namespace. (Ali, 2015; Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b)
DecemberOnename ends support for the old Namecoin /u names. (Cointelegraph, 2015; Myth BTC, n.d.)
2016
JuneThe design is published as a peer-reviewed paper, "Blockstack: A Global Naming and Storage System Secured by Blockchains", at the USENIX Annual Technical Conference. (Ali, Nelson, Shea, & Freedman, 2016)
2017
DecemberBlockstack closes a token sale of about 50 million dollars, with Union Square Ventures and Winklevoss Capital among the backers; 52 million total was reported across the offering. (CoinDesk, 2017; VentureBeat, 2017)
2019
April to SeptemberBlockstack runs the first SEC-qualified Regulation A+ token offering, raising 23 million dollars. (CoinDesk, 2019a; CoinDesk, 2019b)
2020
OctoberBlockstack PBC renames to Hiro Systems; the network and project carry the Stacks name. (Blockstack PBC, 2020; Cointelegraph, 2020)
2021
January 14Stacks 2.0 mainnet launches with Clarity smart contracts; BNS is rewritten as a Clarity contract and every existing name, .id included, is carried into the genesis block for its historical owner. (NewsBTC, 2021; Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b)
JuneThe .btc namespace opens on Stacks, with btc.us as its registrar. (Hiro Systems, n.d.; Stacks Foundation, n.d.-a)
2022
OctoberGamma.io launches a dedicated .btc marketplace, the first large secondary market for BNS names; sales volume there reaches 1.5 million STX, about 2 million dollars. (Gamma.io, 2022; Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b)
2023
Early 2023Nearly 230,000 .btc names are registered, about 86 percent of all BNS names; the BNSx wrapper works around v1's one-name-per-wallet limit, and Leather adds sending Bitcoin to .btc names. (Myth BTC, n.d.)
2024
FebruaryBNS turns ten. (Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b)
SeptemberBNSv2 launches on a September 11 migration snapshot: every name becomes a SIP-009 NFT under one contract, the one-name-per-wallet limit is gone, and zonefiles move on chain into a separate resolver contract. The deployed contract is public: BNS-V2 on the Stacks explorer. (Stacks Foundation, n.d.-b; Trust Machines, n.d.)

Chain-verifiable claims: the /u names sit on Namecoin, the 2015 to 2021 records and the .id burn on Bitcoin, and everything since the 2021 genesis on Stacks, where the BNSv2 contract linked above is the current source of truth.
